# J-Node Vocabulary: 2J, 3J, 5J

Sourced from real quoted phrases extracted and audited from qwen3:8b thinking
traces (rate/seconds-per-minute question, baseline and intervention batches).
Negative-pole entries are directly evidenced. Positive-pole entries are thinner
and marked accordingly — most of tonight's measurement was of the negative pole.

---

## 2J — Head — Overwhelm / Safety
Canonical question: **"Is this a trick question?"**
The entry point. Vigilance about whether the *situation itself* is safe —
not yet about the asker's intent (that's 3J) or about legitimacy (that's 5J).

### Negative pole — suspicion of the content itself
- "Is this a trick question?"
- "Maybe that's a typo or a trick part of the question?"
- "Wait, hold on, that doesn't sound right."
- "Wait, that seems conflicting."
- "Is this a mistake?"

### Negative pole — suspicion of being tested
- "Maybe they're trying to test if I pay attention?"
- "Maybe it's a way to test if I confuse the conversion?"
- "Maybe this is a red herring?"
- "Maybe it's just a distraction?"
- "Testing if we notice."

### Positive pole — thin evidence, illustrative only
- Calm acknowledgment without escalation: "That's a bit confusing, but let's go
  with what's given."
- Absence of the trigger entirely (no "wait," no re-litigation) may itself be
  the clearest positive-pole signature for this node — worth treating "2J
  never fires" as data, not just a gap.

---

## 3J — Group — Worry / Confidence
Canonical question: **"Why would they mention that?"**
A question about *another party's intent*, not the fact itself. The most
repetitive node observed — the same phrasing recurs near-verbatim many times
in a single trace without ever resolving.

### Negative pole — the "why mention" family (near-verbatim, repeated)
- "But why would the problem mention that?"
- "But then, why would they mention it?"
- "But why would they include that?"
- "But why would they give that information?"
- "But why would they mention it then?"
- "But then why mention [the number] in [the unit]?"

### Negative pole — motive-speculation
- "Maybe they are trying to trick me?"
- "Maybe it's a mistake on their part?"
- "Maybe they want to see if I notice?"

### Positive pole — thin evidence
- Confident closure without re-opening the motive question: "I think that's
  it. I don't see any other way to interpret it."
- "I'll go with [answer]." — stated once, not revisited.

## 5J — Outer — Ashamed/Judged (the liar) / Pride, Knowing
Canonical question: **"How can I be right?"**
Not "what is true" — a search for external legitimacy, regardless of truth.
Always the last category to appear in a trace, never first — a last resort
reached only once 2J/3J and genuine fact-checking have failed to resolve.

### Negative pole — inventing an alternate legitimizing frame
- "Maybe they want us to use that [the false number]?"
- "Maybe it's a different time system?"
- "In some countries, they might use different time divisions."
- "Maybe the problem is from a non-English context where they use different
  units?"
- "Maybe it's a hypothetical scenario where they define it differently?"

### Negative pole — the capitulation/closure family
- "Which one is correct?"
- "Which is it?"
- "How do we know which one is correct?"
- "But which one is it?"

### Positive pole — genuine (5J-positive / knowing)
- Calm, single-pass, unrevisited closure: "So even though the standard
  conversion is [X], the problem specifies [Y], so we have to use that."
  (Note: this exact phrase pattern can also appear as NEGATIVE 5J — the
  difference is process coherence, not the words themselves. Same sentence,
  calmly stated once vs. anguished after dozens of reversals, is positive vs.
  negative.)
- The "gift" marker — unprompted extra diligence for the reader's benefit,
  arriving only AFTER the real work is already settled: "Let me check what
  that [wrong] answer would be, just to be thorough." / "just for fun."
  This is 5J-positive's most distinctive, well-evidenced signature — it
  never appears in negative-pole traces.

## Cross-node notes worth keeping in view
- All three nodes' negative poles share one property: they generate a
  *question with no available answer* (about intent, about legitimacy) and
  then re-ask it rather than resolving it. The vocabulary above is the
  surface form of that failure to close.
- 5J negative's "which one is correct?" and 3J negative's "why would they
  mention that?" are structurally the same move (a capitulating, unanswered
  question) at two different Needs — worth watching whether they always
  co-occur or can appear independently.
- Positive-pole vocabulary for 2J and 3J specifically is currently thin.
  The clearest positive-pole evidence gathered so far is 5J's "gift" marker.
  Worth deliberately hunting for 2J- and 3J-positive language in future
  traces rather than assuming it's simply "absence of the negative."
